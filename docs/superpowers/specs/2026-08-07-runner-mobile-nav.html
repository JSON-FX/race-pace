Runner site — mobile navigation

Measured at 375×844, on /events.

Not broken — just out of reach. Unlike the admin console there is no overflow here. The problem is placement: the nav sits 779px from the bottom of the screen, so every move between Races, My Races and Profile is a stretch to the top of a tall phone, and on a signed-in runner those three live behind a hamburger — two taps for the app's primary activity. The logo (30px) and Sign in (40px) are also under the 44px minimum.
Four items, no "More" needed. The runner nav is Home, Races, My Races, Profile — the admin needed a fifth "More" tab to hold ten destinations; this fits the ≤5 rule with room spare, so every destination is exactly one tap. That makes a bottom bar a better fit here than it is on the console.

Event page — Register sits above the bar, not under it
Why the bar earns its place here. Browsing races and pulling up a ticket are the two things runners do on a phone, and both currently cost a reach to the top plus a hamburger tap. A badge on My Races is also the natural home for "your race is in 2 days" — there is nowhere to put that today.

Signed out, the bar shows Home and Races only, with Sign in staying the header CTA — a two-item bar would look broken, and the sign-in prompt belongs where the value is, not as a nav destination.

The event page's Register button is the detail that decides this: it must sit ABOVE the bar, never behind it. That is the classic bottom-nav mistake — a fixed bar covering the one action the page exists for.

Bottom sheet instead of a full-screen menu
The conservative option. Keeps today's structure and just fixes the two undersized targets, swapping the full-screen menu for a bottom sheet so the list itself is within thumb reach even though the trigger isn't. Cheap, and nothing new to maintain — but it leaves the core complaint intact: every navigation still starts with a reach to the top-right corner.
Shared by both, and by the admin's bottom bar. Safe-area inset on the bar and the sheet so nothing sits under the home indicator; aria-current="page" on the active tab; icons paired with labels, never icon-only — an unlabelled tab bar is guesswork on first use; and both stay md:-and-below, so desktop keeps the pill nav it has now.
