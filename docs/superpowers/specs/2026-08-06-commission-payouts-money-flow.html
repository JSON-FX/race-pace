Commission, event-based payouts, and payment method

Race Pace is the merchant of record — every peso lands in the platform's PayMongo account, and the organizer is paid afterwards, per event, net of commission. That makes the payout unit an event, not a calendar month, which is a change from what we agreed earlier.

Commission is per registration, not per batch. This is already how it works — _shared/confirm.ts:26 computes the fee against a single registration's total_amount at confirmation. What is new is the flat-amount option: today organizations.commission_rate is a lone percentage (numeric default 0.10), so a fixed peso fee cannot be expressed.
The rate is frozen per payment. Because the fee is struck and written onto the payment row at confirmation, changing an organization's commission never rewrites past payments — it applies to the next registration onward. Correct for money, but it must be visible on the page or an operator will assume a change is retroactive.

Switching RunWithPoint to a flat ₱75 per registration affects entries paid from now on. Their 702 existing payments keep the 10.0% they were charged at, and the two open payout statements below are unchanged.
Flat fee vs. cheap entries. RunWithPoint's cheapest category is the Pulangi 5K at ₱60. A ₱75 flat fee exceeds it, so that entry would earn the organizer nothing — the fee is clamped to the entry total and never makes net_to_org negative. Shown here because a silent clamp is worse than a visible one.

Why "Held" rather than hidden. An event still taking registrations has a growing net figure, so paying it early means paying twice. Showing it greyed with the reason beats hiding it — the organizer asking "where's my money for Dumalinao?" gets an answer the operator can read off the screen.
Decide: what makes an event payable? The mockup gates on the event being finished (event_date in the past, or status completed). The alternative is gating on registration being closed, which would let you pay before race day. I'd hold until finished, because a refund after a payout is money you have to claw back.

This column needs a backend fix first. admin_payments_v already selects p.method, so the view is fine — but payment-verify/index.ts:52 writes the literal "paymongo" instead of the instrument. The webhook gets it right (payments-webhook/index.ts:28 reads payments[0].attributes.source.type), and payment-verify already fetches that same session — so it is a small extraction fix in one function. Without it this column reads "paymongo" on every redirect-confirmed row.
Backfill. Rows already written as "paymongo" can't be recovered from the DB alone, but each one stores the PayMongo session in payments.raw, so a one-off migration can re-read source.type from there and correct the history. Worth doing while the table is small.
