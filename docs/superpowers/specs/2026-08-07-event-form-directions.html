Event form — three directions

Measured on the edit screen for Dulang-Dulang Vertical, at 1280px and 375px.

What's wrong now. 28 of 30 form controls are 36px tall, under the 44px touch minimum — on mobile the whole form is a 2,308px scroll of them, roughly three screens with no structure and no sense of what's required. On desktop the Categories table squeezes price and slots until "2100" reads as "210" and "1500" as "150", and its headers wrap to three lines ("ELEVATION GAIN (M, OPTIONAL)"). Labels are 11px ALL-CAPS, which is the hardest case to scan quickly. Nothing anywhere says which sections still need attention.

Mobile — the rail becomes an accordion
Why this one. The rail is the only option that answers "what still needs doing?" without scrolling — the dots and counts show at a glance that Schedule, Inclusions and Add-ons are empty. A race director editing a live event usually wants one section, but has to be able to trust that the rest is intact.

The Categories table becomes cards, which is what fixes the truncated money. A table forces every column to share one width; a card gives price and slots a full field each, and it is the same component on mobile instead of a horizontally-scrolling table.

On mobile the rail collapses to an accordion with the same dots, so the mental model does not change between devices — and only one section is open, which takes the page from a 2,308px scroll to roughly one screen.

The sticky save bar replaces a Save button stranded at the bottom of three screens, and states unsaved changes explicitly — a long form is exactly where someone navigates away mid-edit.

Each tab is a short, focused pane
Cleanest single screen, riskiest for a form. Every pane is short and nothing competes for attention. But a validation error on a tab you are not looking at is invisible — the operator hits Save and gets "fix the errors" with no idea where. It also hides how much is left: with everything behind tabs, an event missing its categories looks identical to a complete one until you click through. On mobile the tab strip has to scroll horizontally, which reintroduces the "destinations you cannot see" problem.

Same order, just bigger and grouped
The minimum honest fix. Keeps today's structure and order — nothing to relearn — but raises every control to 44px, converts the ALL-CAPS micro-labels to sentence case at a readable size, groups fields under real headings, and adds the sticky save bar. It does not solve the two structural complaints: the mobile page is still a very long scroll, and there is still no way to see what is incomplete without reading all of it.

Worth choosing if the priority is "stop it being painful" rather than "rethink it".
Common to all three. Controls to 44px on touch (Apple HIG / Material both require it, and a date field at 36px is genuinely hard to hit); labels in sentence case with a real required marker rather than ALL-CAPS; helper text below the field it explains rather than after it; the first invalid field focused on a failed submit; and the map/GPX editors given a full-width row of their own instead of sharing a squeezed column.
